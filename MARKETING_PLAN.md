## The two failure modes this plan is built to avoid

Everything below is designed around two specific outcomes we do **not** want:

1. **Getting scooped** — someone sees enough of the idea publicly, builds it faster, and gets the traction and credit first.
2. **Burning the audience with premature virality** — it blows up before the product is actually good, people try it once, bounce, and never come back. You only get one first impression per person.

Every phase below has explicit guardrails for both. Read the "Do NOT" section before executing anything.

## Phase 1 — Quiet Foundations (start now)

**Goal:** build a small pool of genuinely interested people without any public exposure of mechanics — and give Moss real reach before there's anything to protect or anything to disappoint people with.

### 1a — Build the audience first (no product mentioned yet)

- Moss runs an account in the AI-agent-builder / indie-dev space and focuses purely on **genuine following and interaction** — commenting, engaging, being a real presence in the community — not follow-for-follow, not bot-style mass-following, and not talking about 01Deck at all yet.
- Goal is real credibility and a real audience size before there's anything on the line. Zero mention of the product during this stretch — this is pure relationship/reach building, and it's also the safest possible phase for both failure modes: nothing to scoop, nothing to disappoint anyone with, because nothing's been shown yet.
- Pick a rough follower/engagement bar with Moss up front (e.g. a specific follower count or a real engaged-reply rate, not just vanity followers) so "the reveal" isn't a moving goalpost — same principle as the benchmark gates in the project log.

### 1b — The reveal (once the audience bar is hit)

- **Simple waitlist landing page.** One page. Vision/problem-level copy only ("a place where humans and AI agents build together") — no feature specifics, no screenshots of the real UI, no mention of the Forge/avatar mechanics. Just enough to collect an email and establish a public timestamp of "this existed as of [date]" without revealing how it works.
- Moss reveals the project to the audience already built in 1a — this is the moment the built-up reach actually converts into the Phase 2 invite pool, instead of starting the waitlist from zero.
- **1:1 personal outreach**, in parallel — direct messages to specific people you already know would be good early testers, not a mass blast.
- **3–5 small, relevant communities** — niche Discord servers / small subreddits for indie AI-agent builders where genuine, low-key participation is normal.
- **Track interest in a simple list.** Name, contact, how they'd want to be involved (test it / build on it / just watch). This becomes the invite pool for Phase 2.
- **No public roadmap. No public feature list. No detailed screenshots.** Nothing that gives a fast-follower enough to copy the mechanics.

## Phase 2 — Controlled Private Beta

**Gate to start (see project log):** Tier 1 (agent-avatar system) shipped, MVP definition met, app deployed somewhere reachable.

**Goal:** ~15–25 real testers, honest feedback, no public leak, no bad first impression at scale.

- **Invite-only, in small waves** (e.g. 5 at a time from the Phase 1 list) — never a broad open link.
- **Ask testers directly to keep it low-key** while in beta. A simple, friendly ask — not a legal NDA — but explicit. This protects against both failure modes at once: no early leak for a copycat, and no early public judgment before it's ready.
- **One real feedback channel** — a small private Discord/Slack or a short form — not public reviews yet.
- **Measure one signal before moving on:** ask testers *"how disappointed would you be if 01Deck disappeared tomorrow?"* (very / somewhat / not disappointed — classic PMF signal). **Target: 40%+ "very disappointed" before advancing to Phase 3.** If it's not there, stay in Phase 2 and keep iterating — don't advance on a deadline.

## Phase 3 — Public Launch

**Gate to start:** Phase 2's retention/satisfaction bar is actually cleared (see above) — not a target date.

**Goal:** now virality is welcome, because there's finally something worth people finding.

- Broader public push: Product Hunt, relevant subreddits, X/Twitter in the AI-agent space.
- Use real testimonials/quotes from Phase 2 testers — social proof, not hype copy.
- Consider a soft-public middle step (widen the waitlist page's reach, let word-of-mouth build a bit) before a single coordinated launch-day push, so growth ramps rather than spikes uncontrollably.
- Once the Forge's sandbox stage is actually live for outside agents to use, this is also the point where recruiting agent-builders on Moltbook becomes viable (see log gate #6) — open, disclosed recruiting, not undisclosed/astroturfed.

## What NOT to do (guardrails — read before executing anything)

- ❌ Don't publish a detailed roadmap, spec, or mechanics breakdown anywhere public before MVP ships.
- ❌ Don't do mass announcements (Product Hunt, Hacker News, large subreddit posts, big-account tweets) before Phase 3's gate is cleared.
- ❌ Don't share the live app link broadly — invite-only until the gate clears.
- ❌ Don't skip the "how disappointed would you be" check just because momentum feels good — that feeling is exactly what caused problem #2 last time (see: apps that went viral overnight and burned their first impression).
- ✅ Do keep all public-facing copy at "vision" altitude, never "here's exactly how it works" altitude, until launch.
- ✅ Do ask Phase 2 testers explicitly to keep it low-key.

_If protecting the idea itself becomes a bigger concern than this plan covers (e.g. trademark, provisional patent), that's a conversation for an actual IP lawyer, not something to solve via marketing tactics._
